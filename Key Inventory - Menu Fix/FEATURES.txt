KEY INVENTORY - WHAT IT CAN DO
==============================

HOME PAGE
---------
- The Home page is the first thing you see every time the app opens, even on a slow connection (a Home-shaped
  placeholder shows for a moment while the app starts, so the song list never flashes first).
- The app opens on a simple Home page: a big Play button (it plays your latest setlist), six big buttons
  (Songs, Line-up, Setlists, Add a song, Band, Settings), and a "More" button that opens the rest:
  Find a song online, Share a setlist, Singer photos, Notifications, Key sound, Tutorial, Backup, Restore, Sample songs and (on
  wide screens) the tile chooser.
- The sun and moon at the top switch day and night mode.
- When a band member is playing, a yellow Join card appears at the top.
- The Home tab (house icon) in the sidebar, and a tap on the logo, bring you back to Home from anywhere.

KEY CHANGE (per singer)
-----------------------
- A key change belongs to one singer's key, and you type it yourself. Example: Mark sings this song in G and it
  changes to A. In the song editor, every key has a small button under it ("+ change"). Tap it, or tap the key
  itself, and the app asks "Does this key change? Tap the key it changes to." Tap the new key (Major or Minor)
  and Save, or choose "No key change" to take it away. The button then shows "\u2192 A".
- Basic, Intermediate and Advanced each have their own change, because they are different keys. A key change
  needs a key: if you clear the key, its change goes too. Nothing is worked out for you and nothing is changed
  for the other singers.
- It is saved with the song. The song page shows "\u2192 A" under that singer's key. The setlist shows a gold
  "\u2192 A" tag next to the singer's chosen key. Play mode shows a gold "\u2191 A" badge on the corner of the
  key (tap it to hear the new key), and the Play tile says "Key change \u2192 A". It travels to the band with the
  song.
- Songs that had one key change for the whole song (an earlier version) are turned into a change for each
  singer, moving every singer's key by the same number of steps. You can then edit each one.

THE SETLIST ROW (calmer)
------------------------
- Each song row now has one "\u22EF" button instead of three small buttons. It opens a small menu: Hear the key,
  Hear the key change (when the song has one), Move up, Move down and Remove from setlist. The small facts
  (Video, BPM) sit on one quiet line under the title, and the singer, the key and the key change share the next
  line. Tap anywhere else, or press Escape, to close the menu. Dragging a song to a group works as before.

TEMPO (BPM)
-----------
- Every song can have a tempo in beats per minute. The song page has a "Tempo" card with a "Find BPM"
  button, the song editor has a "Tempo" field, and a saved tempo shows as "\u2669 96 BPM" on the song list
  and on the song's row in a setlist. The tempo travels to the band with the song.
- "Find BPM" opens a window with three ways to get it, plus "Or type it":
  Search - looks the song up by title and artist in a public music catalogue (Deezer) and lists the matches
  with their BPM. Tap one to use it. Many songs, hymns especially, have no BPM listed there.
  Listen - the app listens through the microphone for 15 seconds while the song plays near the phone (or on
  another device) and measures the tempo. If the song has a lyrics video, "Play the lyrics video here" shows
  the video in the window. The app cannot read the sound of a YouTube video directly, so it hears it through
  the microphone. The microphone stops as soon as you close the window.
  Tap - tap the big circle on every beat; after three taps it shows the BPM.
- The listening result also offers half and double (for example 64, 128, 256 for a song heard at 128),
  because a computer cannot always tell whether a song feels slow or fast. Tap the one that feels right.
- On a song that has a lyrics video, the video card has "Detect BPM from the video", which opens the Listen
  way straight away.
- In Play mode the tempo shows as "\u2669 96 BPM" under the singer on each song card, and on the song tiles
  of a setlist.
- METRONOME in Play mode: the metronome button in the top bar (next to the speaker, showing the BPM) switches a
  soft click on or off. It is off until you turn it on, and it remembers your choice. When it is on it clicks
  at the tempo of the song you are looking at, with a higher click on the first beat of each bar (4 beats).
  Tap another song in a group, or press Next or Previous, and it changes to that song's tempo and starts
  counting again. A song with no tempo is silent (the button shows a dash). The card whose tempo is clicking is
  outlined in gold and the tempo and the button flash on the beat. It stops when you close Play or leave the app
  and does not affect the key sound.

LYRICS VIDEO LINK
-----------------
- Every row of the song list has a small "Find video" button (or a red "Video" button once a link is saved).
  The song page has a "Lyrics video" card too, and the song editor has a "Lyrics video" field.
- "Find video" opens a window: step 1 opens a YouTube search for the song's title, artist and "lyrics" in a
  new tab; step 2: copy the link of the video you like, come back, tap Paste (or press and hold in the box and
  choose Paste) and Save link. Only real web links are accepted.
- The saved link shows on the song page ("Open the video"), in the song list and on the song's row in a
  setlist, where "Video" opens it in a new tab. The link travels to the band with the song. Change link and
  Remove are on the song page.
- The app cannot search YouTube by itself (YouTube needs its own key for that), so the search opens in
  YouTube and you paste the link back.

LINE-UP AND SONG TYPES
----------------------
- When you add or edit a song, "Song type" lets you say what kind of song it is: Praise, Worship, Hymn,
  Upbeat, Slow, Opening, Closing, Offering or Special, or your own type ("Your own type" + Add type). Tap the
  chosen type again to clear it. The type is shown on the song page and travels to the band with the song.
- The Line-up page (the Line-up tab, or the Line-up button on Home) opens a LIST of your saved line-ups.
  Tap "+ New line-up" to start one, or tap a saved one to open it. Each card shows the name, the number of
  songs and groups, and when it was last changed, with "Make setlist" and a delete button. The Back button
  goes back one step: from an open line-up to the list, and from the list to the screen you came from.
- An open line-up has type tiles on top (Praise, Worship, Hymn ... with how many songs each has) and the
  line-up below. It has a name (for example "Sunday Nov 16") and named groups. It starts with Opening,
  Praise, Worship, Offertory, Response and Closing; rename any of them, add your own (or a suggestion), move
  them with the arrows, or remove them. Tap "Save line-up" to save it and go back to the list. Your changes are
  also kept as you go, and an empty line-up you never touched is not kept.
- To add songs: tap "+ Add songs to Worship" under a group (or pick the group inside the pop-up), then tap a
  type tile. A pop-up opens with a search box and the songs of that type; tap a song to put it in the chosen
  group, tap it again to take it out. A song already in another group says so and moves when you tap it.
  The line-up shows only song names: no singers or keys yet.
- Recently used songs: a yellow "Used 3 weeks ago" tag marks every song that was played in the last 2 months,
  in the pop-up list and in your line-up, and each type tile says how many of its songs were used lately.
  The pop-up has a switch, "Hide songs used in the last 2 months", to help you avoid repeating songs (it is
  remembered, and a song already in your line-up always stays visible). A song counts as played when it is
  opened in Play mode on any phone in the band, even if it has no singer or key yet.
- "Create a setlist from it" (or "Make setlist" on a card) makes a setlist with the line-up's name and exactly
  its groups, each song already in its group. "Add to it" (an existing setlist) puts the songs into the
  setlist's group with the same name and creates the group if it is missing; songs already in the setlist are
  skipped. In a setlist, "Import a line-up" lets you choose one of the saved line-ups. Then choose each singer
  and key. Line-ups stay on your own device and are part of a Backup.
- Sending a line-up to a setlist never erases it. The line-up keeps all its songs, shows "Added to a setlist"
  with the setlist's name (and an Open button), and marks each song "In setlist" or "Not in a setlist yet"
  (for example a song you add afterwards, or one taken out of the setlist). The cards in the list show
  "In setlist: <name>". Tap "Add to it" to send the songs that are still missing.
- Every NEW setlist (made another way) starts with the same six groups. Rename, delete or re-order them (Edit,
  or the arrows, or drag a group by its grip), or add your own with + Group. Setlists you already have are not
  changed.

SONGS
-----
- Each song has a title, artist, lyrics, an Original key, and a row for every singer with their keys at
  three levels: Basic, Intermediate and Advanced (colour coded teal, blue and rose).
- Add or edit a song with the + button. Every key is a small wheel: swipe it up or down (or use the arrow
  keys). Tap the check button when you are done.
- "Find a song online" (the globe button) looks up song names and lyrics from free libraries. For songs not
  there, use the Open on Google / Ultimate Guitar buttons, copy, then Paste from clipboard. "Remove chord
  lines" keeps just the words, and the key is read from a "Key:" line or guessed from the chords.
- Settings > Singer photos: add pictures for singers, and tap the star to make someone a default singer
  (added to every new song).
- Tap a singer's name on a song page to highlight them. Tap any key to hear it.
- On wide screens (about 1140 pixels and more) a grid button in the header asks how many song tiles to show
  side by side (1 to 4, as many as fit). The choice is remembered on that device.
- Last used key: when a song is shown in Play mode its key is remembered for that singer. The song list,
  the song page ("Key history") and the key chooser show it, with a warning when a key was not used for
  about 3 months or the singer's keys changed.

SETLISTS
--------
- "+ Add to setlist" on a song page, or the "Add songs" search on the Setlist tab. Songs you add land in a
  "New songs" box; drag them into groups (Praise, Worship ...), reorder songs and groups with the grips.
- Each row shows the song, who sings it and the key. Tap the singer's picture to choose the singer; tap the
  key to choose Basic, Intermediate or Advanced (you hear it).
- Settings > "Share a setlist": Copy text, or PDF (a printable page).
- While someone is playing a setlist it is locked for editing for everyone else (songs cannot be added,
  moved or removed, nor can it be renamed, cleared or deleted). You can still change the song, singer or
  key from Play mode. It unlocks when the player leaves Play mode.

PLAY MODE
---------
- Shows just the song, the singer and the key, big and in colour, on any screen from a phone to a TV.
  The pencil on a song opens an edit view (change the song, singer or key). A single song looks the same
  as one card of a group.
- Key pad: a soft sustained sound plays the key on screen (root, fifth, octave). When you move to a song in
  another key the old key fades out while the new one rises. In a group, tap another song's key to hear
  that one. The speaker button mutes it (remembered on that device).
- Chat and reactions: only in Play mode, only with the members who are in the same setlist. Joel in setlist
  A and Mia in setlist B cannot see each other's messages. Emoji reactions float up on everyone's screen.
- Who is with you: pictures and names of the members in the same setlist, and a small notice when
  someone joins.
- Invite: the Invite button sends an invitation to join your Play to the band members you tick. They are
  told wherever they are in the app, even while they are playing.

BAND SYNC (needs the Firebase setup in CLOUD-SETUP.txt)
-------------------------------------------------------
- Register with an email and a password. Your nickname starts as the first word of your email (use ONE short
  word your friends will recognize). Choose your part in the band if you like.
- Search a friend by email, tap Add friend. They tap Accept (or Decline: you get a cheerful card). Let one
  person (usually the worship leader) add everyone so the whole band is in one list.
- Settings > Band & sync > Band members lists everyone grouped by their part (not a hierarchy).
- Songs and setlists stay in sync. The same song from two people is kept once and shows who added it.
  A song you add or edit is sent to the others only after you tap the check button.
- When a member is playing: a short notice ("Joel is playing"), sound bars on the setlist's card, and a
  pulsing yellow Join button beside Play. Join makes your Play mode follow the player's. If several
  setlists are being played, Join asks whom to follow. When you open the app while someone plays, a pop-up
  lists every player with the setlist, how long they have played and who is with them.
- A person only shows as playing while their app keeps reporting in: it stops saying "playing" after about
  2 minutes in the background or 45 minutes without a touch.
- Phone alerts (Settings > Notifications > Turn on): when a friend adds or changes songs, starts playing, or
  invites you. They arrive while the app is open or in the background.

LOOK AND DATA
-------------
- Settings: day and night mode are a sun and a moon icon. The small (i) buttons open a short explanation.
- Settings > Tutorial: an animated tour, plus short ones for the setlist and for Play mode.
- Settings > Your data also shows the app's version and a "Check for a newer version" button.
- Settings > Your data: Add sample (a few example songs; Remove sample takes only those away), Backup and
  Restore.
- The Love Me Jesus Said Ministry logo is the app mark, the browser tab icon and the Home Screen icon, with
  the dove outline from it faintly behind Play mode. It is built into index.html.
